\section{\makebox[\widthof{\faFlask}][c]{\color{CVBlue}\faFlask}\ Proposed Research Direction}
During the summer research internship at Carnegie Mellon University from July 6, 2026 to September 4, 2026, I plan to conduct research in computer science under the guidance of Associate Professor Lei Li. The proposed research direction is \textbf{learning from feedback for multi-modal and agentic large language models}. This direction is closely related to large language models, multi-modal learning, and agent-based systems, and it aims to study how feedback signals and training objectives shape the reasoning and decision-making behavior of modern AI systems.

The project will focus on how large language models can learn from different forms of feedback, including human preference, model-generated judgments, task rewards, trajectory-level evaluations, and multi-modal observations such as screenshots or visual states. A central goal is to understand how these feedback signals influence model reasoning, task completion, and reliability in practical interactive environments.

My previous research experience has prepared me for this topic. In \textbf{TimeThinkingLLM}, I studied how continuous time-series signals can be aligned with language-model reasoning through temporal encoders, chain-of-thought data, and reinforcement learning methods such as GRPO. In \textbf{CUARewarder}, I worked on trajectory evaluation and reward modeling for GUI agents, using visual-language models and judge backends to assess whether agents complete web or desktop tasks. In \textbf{TradeActor}, I built a multi-modal forecasting and policy-learning pipeline involving signal generation, model-based evaluation, teacher-label construction, policy training, and backtesting. These projects provide a foundation for the proposed research on feedback-driven learning and evaluation for language-model agents.

The main research questions include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item How do different feedback signals affect the reasoning behavior and task performance of large language models?
    \item How can multi-modal inputs, such as screenshots, visual states, structured records, and action histories, be incorporated into model training and evaluation?
    \item How can agent-based systems be evaluated beyond final-answer matching, especially when task success depends on intermediate actions and environmental state changes?
    \item What training objectives can improve model reliability while keeping experiments reproducible and scientifically meaningful?
\end{itemize}

\section{\makebox[\widthof{\faTasks}][c]{\color{CVBlue}\faTasks}\ Training Plan and Timeline}
\textbf{Weeks 1--2: Orientation and literature review.} I will become familiar with the host research environment, meet with the supervisor and research group, and review recent work on large language models, multi-modal learning, reinforcement learning from feedback, agentic LLMs, and trustworthy generative AI. Based on discussion with the supervisor, I will define a concrete research problem and set up the required codebase, datasets, and experimental environment.

\textbf{Weeks 3--4: Baseline implementation.} I will implement or reproduce relevant state-of-the-art methods, prepare datasets or task environments, build evaluation scripts, and establish baseline results. This stage will focus on correctness, reproducibility, and understanding the strengths and limitations of existing feedback-learning and agent-evaluation methods.

\textbf{Weeks 5--7: Experimentation and method development.} I will conduct validation experiments, compare different feedback designs and training objectives, analyze model behavior, and propose possible technical improvements. Depending on the progress of the project, this work may involve reward modeling, judge-based evaluation, trajectory-level supervision, reinforcement learning from feedback, or integration of multi-modal observations into the learning process.

\textbf{Weeks 8--9: Analysis, writing, and presentation.} I will summarize the experimental results, prepare figures and tables, analyze the findings and limitations, and write a final research report or paper draft. I will also present the project goals, methods, results, and conclusions to the supervisor and research group. If the results are promising, I plan to continue improving the work after the internship toward submission to an appropriate academic venue.
